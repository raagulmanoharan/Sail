# Sail identity concepts

Three original native SVG identity studies, with outlined DM Sans Bold wordmarks. No external fonts, generated imagery, gradients or raster textures are required. DM Sans license remains in the project's `assets/dm-sans-OFL.txt`.

## Recommendation: 01 / Open sail

One continuous triangular silhouette with a sharp, asymmetric negative-space seam. The seam suggests an opening and the folded plane suggests forward movement, without using a chat bubble, sparkle or literal boat. The icon remains legible at 24px and reverses cleanly to white. It is considerably more decisive than the existing two soft stripes, which can read as a flag, wave or generic AI ribbon.

The icon and wordmark should be navy on pale backgrounds and white on navy. Use the existing blue as an action accent elsewhere; avoid making the identity itself a blue gradient. Minimum icon size: 20px. Allow clear space of at least 12/80 of the icon width. For in-reel system nodes use the icon at 64px or greater and keep the wordmark horizontal. For the closing frame use the complete lockup with generous breathing room, rather than a floating icon badge.

## 02 / Threshold

A substantial open frame with a diagonal wedge crossing its boundary. This feels more technical and infrastructural and renders well at small sizes. The drawback is that it can resemble an external-link or doorway symbol. A credible alternative if the brand should feel closer to business software than customer experience.

## 03 / Relay

A continuous curved path with a lower receiving segment. It evokes a hand-off and is the heaviest of the three silhouettes. Its limitation is that some readers may see a numeral 2 or letter R, so it is less suitable as the primary Sail identity.

## Files

Each concept has `-icon.svg`, `-lockup.svg`, and `-light.svg`. `logo-board.png` shows large lockups, 24px and 48px marks, and reversed lockups. `preview.html` and `preview.mjs` rebuild the board. Wordmark text is converted to vector paths, not dependent on browser text layout.

These are visual concept studies. No trademark availability claim is made; a selected direction should receive a formal identity and trademark review before commercial launch.
