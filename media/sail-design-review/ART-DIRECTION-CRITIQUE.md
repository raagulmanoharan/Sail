# Independent graphic art-direction review

Reviewed the current screen 5 at 40.5s, finale at 56.7s, and the six-screen revision board. This is a graphic-design review of the actual screenshots, not a validation of the integration architecture.

## Overall verdict

The reel has a coherent visual language now. Real imagery, the persistent silver phone, ice backgrounds and compact cards make the offering understandable. Keep those. The remaining issue is hierarchy: the diagram asks the eye to read too many equally styled containers, and the finale enlarges everything at once instead of making one unmistakable hero. This needs two focused adjustments, not another wholesale redesign.

## Screen 5: make the system-to-experience relationship legible

**What works:** The systems panel is readable. The phone is large enough to identify the delivered branded experience. The selected document workflow links the diagram to something already demonstrated.

**What weakens it:** The 480px-wide Sail panel has a dark masthead followed by three stacked framed panels, producing four tiers of boxes inside another box. Its weight exceeds the phone. The long connector bends down the side of the panel, making the journey feel routed around the content. A large blank band separates the diagram from the caption. `Status: received` is a disproportionately prominent blue monospace line, so a minor example reads as the diagram's main subject.

**Strongest fix:** Keep the systems bar across the top. Turn the Sail area into a single shallow module on the left: logo above two vertically compact rows, **Connected actions** and **Branded components**. Use a slim lavender footer labeled **Assistant adapters**, rather than a third card of equal depth. Align the centre of this module with the branded widget on the right. Connect with one short horizontal blue line that lands on the widget edge, not the device frame. Move the whole working composition downward approximately 90–130px so it centres in the visual field without increasing its height.

**Styling details:** Retain navy, ice and lavender; use green only for an actual successful state. Reduce the `Status: received` example to 20–22px muted text with a tiny green check. Remove one level of outer framing or its shadow. Keep one strong shadow on the phone; the diagram should have a quieter 1px outline. Preserve current type sizes for headings, but trim repeated explanatory copy to one short line per row.

## Finale: keep the richness, restore a hero

**What works:** Four completed experiences communicate the generic offering far better than a hotel-only close. The photo cards add credibility. The headline is memorable. The phone is clearly the place these moments happen.

**What weakens it:** At 56.7s the composition is crowded vertically: a very large two-line headline, phone, four tilted cards, large logo and another sentence at the bottom compete. The left hotel card and right watch card touch or overlap the device outline; that can read as a collision rather than a intentional fan. The bottom logo and caption leave almost no calm landing space. The cheap hotel-room photo and close-up black watch crop also look less aspirational than the headline implies.

**Strongest fix:** Make the phone the hero and treat the outer confirmations as a halo, not four equal competing posters. Reduce the headline approximately 12–15%, and reduce the floating-card text approximately 8–10%. Keep cards on two consistent widths and use matched image heights for the hotel and product. Give the phone a 20–30px visible moat wherever a card meets it. Keep the seat and document cards 15% smaller than the photo cards. The four confirmations should appear to complete one connected arc; do not add more ornaments.

**Closing motion direction:** First, the phone's confirmed state settles. Then the four cards fan into place from that same widget centre along short curved paths, with checkmarks resolving in sequence. Only after the halo is established should the Sail logo arrive and the headline resolve. Hold the clean final arrangement for at least 1.2 seconds. One continuous expansion feels celebratory; five separate entrance animations will bring back the disconnected slide-in problem.

**Final-frame hierarchy:** `Make the moment yours.` → one branded experience in the phone → confirmation halo → small Sail lockup. Keep the spoken caption but raise it slightly into the safe area and avoid treating it as a second marketing headline. Use the same navy as the phone text, a subtle lavender radial field, and no new saturated colours.

## Logo direction

The existing two-wave flag is readable but visually anonymous; it can resemble stacked ribbons, an abstract flag, or a generic financial-app mark. It does not have a strongly ownable silhouette when reduced to the diagram's small masthead. Keep the wordmark straightforward. Test a single continuous sail-shaped silhouette with a deliberate negative-space cut that suggests a forward opening or an S. It must work in one colour at 24px, with no gradients, no faux 3D and no extra sparkles. Select a mark by small-size recognition, not by the size of the presentation board.

## Keep unchanged

- The plain-text recommendation versus branded interactive widget contrast.
- Matching customer requests when each use case changes.
- Compact catalogue and seat-selection cards.
- The persistent phone's chrome and existing caption typography.
- The restrained ice/navy/lavender palette and real photographs.
